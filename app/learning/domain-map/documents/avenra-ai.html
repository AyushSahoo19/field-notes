01 Site Why here

An unlikely place,
for a specific reason

Chokepoint technology is overwhelmingly physical. Jamshedpur is one of very few Indian cities where heavy industry, engineering talent and low cost of living overlap in the same square kilometre. Don't build a worse Bangalore — build the thing that is only possible here.

Industrial base
Adityapur

One of eastern India's largest MSME clusters. Auto ancillaries, precision machining, foundries, tooling — real machines with real defects and real measurement problems.

Talent base
NIT · XLRI

Metallurgy, mechanical, electrical and electronics next door to a top business school. Rare to have a factory belt and industrial economics in the same city.

Corridor
440 km east

Odisha now hosts heterogeneous-integration packaging and silicon carbide capacity. Assam hosts assembly and test. This is a corridor, not a set of islands.

The "India needs a fab" era is closing. Assembly and test plants are operating, and the country's first logic fab is heading toward trial production at mature nodes. The bottleneck moved downstream.

Even when the fab runs, it runs on imported equipment, imported specialty chemicals, imported electronic-grade gases and imported materials.

So the right agenda isn't "learn about fabs." It's design and IP, metrology and inspection, advanced packaging, and materials.

Two of those four are where a Jamshedpur community beats a Bangalore one outright — because both are built on measurement, materials and precision engineering this city already does every day.

02 Atlas Ten domains

The chokepoint atlas

A chokepoint technology is foundational, bottlenecked, concentrated in few hands, import-dependent for India, skill-compounding, and uncrowded. Anything passing four of those six is work worth doing here. Tiered by what it costs to start.

CodeDomainTierCapitalTests

India holds a very large share of the world's chip design workforce, but the overwhelming majority is service work inside multinational captives — verification and physical design on someone else's architecture, no IP ownership. Almost nobody in India takes a design end-to-end and owns it.

Verilog and SystemVerilog, RISC-V cores, open EDA flows, open PDKs, low-cost multi-project shuttle runs.

12-month goal — one design taped out. One chip in one hand beats a hundred meetups.

Millions of Indians use ML frameworks. A vanishingly small number can write a fused kernel, extend a compiler dialect, or reason about memory hierarchy. The highest-paid, most globally scarce software skill that needs zero capital to learn.

Every industrial vision project eventually hits the wall of "this must run on a ₹15,000 edge box on a shop floor in 30 milliseconds." That wall is a compiler problem.

Entry artifact — a custom kernel measurably faster than the framework default

Verification consumes the majority of effort in any serious chip or safety-critical project. In India it's treated as labour arbitrage rather than an intellectual discipline, so depth is rare and expensive.

The cheapest chokepoint in existence — a laptop and a very specific kind of patience. Also the technical prerequisite for trusting any system that runs without supervision.

Entry artifact — a machine-checked proof of a non-trivial property

One of the world's largest producers of steel, aluminium and cement is almost entirely a licence-buyer of simulation software. We don't build solvers.

Density functional theory, molecular dynamics, finite elements, phase-field methods, materials informatics. Local fit is extremely high — a steel plant next door and a metallurgy department at NIT make alloy design legible here in a way they are nowhere else in India.

Entry artifact — a validated simulation of a real alloy or process

Spectrometers, interferometers, profilometers, electron microscopes, coordinate measuring machines — almost entirely imported. Every Indian lab, every fab, every QA line depends on a supply chain the country has no position in. Arguably India's most systemically important and least discussed technology gap.

This is the signature domain. It is the exact bridge between what Jamshedpur already does — visual inspection and dimensional QA on steel — and what the semiconductor industry needs: wafer and package inspection, defect classification, in-line metrology. Nobody else in India stands on both sides of that bridge.

Entry artifact — a working instrument with a calibration record

India imports the large majority of its power modules, and wide-bandgap capability is barely nascent. Meanwhile every growth story in the country — electric vehicles, solar, grid storage, data centres, rail, industrial drives — is gated on power electronics.

A steel plant is a power electronics museum. Rolling mill drives, arc furnace supplies, motor systems — with real failure data attached.

Entry artifact — a converter running under load

Near-total import dependence in India. Globally, optical interconnect is becoming the hard scaling wall for AI datacentres — electrical input and output are running out of headroom, and co-packaged optics is the frontier.

Intimidating, but a usable optics bench costs a few lakh and the simulation half is free.

Entry artifact — a functioning optical measurement setup

India has an enormous embedded workforce and shallow control-theoretic depth. Most Indian embedded work is driver plumbing, not control design, and capability in certifiable hard-real-time systems is rare.

Process control is the daily work of a steel plant. There is no better classroom.

Entry artifact — a controlled physical system with a stability proof

Advanced packaging capacity is the global constraint on AI hardware supply. Simultaneously, virtually all of India's approved semiconductor capacity is assembly, test and packaging rather than front-end fabrication. This is where Indian jobs, revenue and strategic position actually are — and almost no Indian curriculum teaches it.

The Odisha heterogeneous-packaging project is 440 km away. Begin the relationship early. A two-year courtship, not a cold email.

Year 3 — a characterised package with a thermal model validated on hardware

India has close to no commercial supply chain here. Yet quantum computing, superconductivity, space systems, fusion research, electron microscopy, MRI and semiconductor fabrication all sit on top of cryogenics and vacuum technology. People in India who can design a working vacuum chamber number in the low hundreds.

Hardest to start, most defensible if you do.

How to use this

Robotics spans L1–L7 in a single enclosure. IoT is L1, L5 and L6 with a thin L7. Industry 4.0 is the integration of L4 through L8 — which is why it so often stalls at L5, the layer nobody owns. Mechatronics is L2–L4 taught as one subject.

Every project on this page is tagged with the layers it touches. If your work only ever touches L7 and L8, that is worth knowing about yourself.

The uncomfortable read

Almost every Indian AI startup occupies L7 and L8 only. That is a real business, but it is two layers of nine, and every one of them is defended by a model that any competitor can retrain. The differentiated position — for a company or a career — is holding one deep layer nobody else wants to touch, and one shallow layer where the money is.

These occupancy readings are ours, drawn as teaching examples. Argue with them. That argument is the Frontier session.

07 Mirror PR-17 · PR-18

Build the factory
twice

Once in steel, once in geometry. A digital twin is usually sold as a dashboard — live tags on a screen — which is the shallowest version of the idea and the least useful. The version that pays is geometric and sensor-accurate: a 3D reconstruction of a real cell, complete with materials, lighting and camera optics, in which you can author events that have never happened, render them with perfect labels, and train against them before a single real example exists. It is the most direct answer this community has to the no-data regime.

Twin fidelity Five levels, and most people stop at one
F0
Dashboard twin

Live tag values on a screen. Useful for operators, useless for training anything. This is what most vendors mean by "digital twin."

PR-04 · Q4
F1
Geometric twin

Accurate 3D of the cell — machine, guarding, conveyor, fixtures, camera mounts. Built from CAD where it exists, photogrammetry or laser scan where it doesn't. Suddenly you can answer "where would the camera go" without visiting.

PR-17 · Q2
F2
Kinematic twin

Things move correctly. Joints, cycle times, conveyor speeds, robot reach, collisions. Now you can test a layout change or an inspection trigger point before anyone stops the line.

PR-17 · Q3
F3
Sensor-accurate twin

Simulated cameras with real lens parameters, real exposure, real noise. Physically-based materials — oxide, scale, oil film, machined aluminium. Lighting that matches the actual fixtures. This is the level where synthetic training data starts to transfer.

PR-17 · Q4
F4
Predictive twin

Closed loop. The twin runs ahead of the real cell, a policy is trained inside it, and the reality gap is measured and fed back. The point at which the simulation earns the word "twin."

PR-18 · Year 2
The synthetic data loop Why this solves the no-data problem
01
Capture

CAD, photogrammetry, or laser scan of the real cell and the parts moving through it.

02
Assemble

Scene built in Blender or OpenUSD. Materials, lighting rig, camera placed where the real one is.

03
Author the defect

Model the flaw that happens twice a year — the one you have no photographs of. This step is the whole reason.

04
Randomise

Vary lighting, pose, surface finish, dust, exposure, focus. Domain randomisation is what makes it generalise.

05
Render

Ten thousand images with pixel-perfect masks, depth, and normals. Labels are free because the renderer knows the truth.

06
Train

Pretrain on synthetic, fine-tune on the handful of real frames you do have.

07
Measure the gap

Validate on real data and report the sim-to-real drop honestly. Unmeasured, this whole pipeline is a graphics demo.

Simulated sensors What each one actually needs
RGB cameraTractable

Lens model, sensor noise, exposure, rolling shutter, and physically-based materials. The gap is usually lighting and surface finish, not geometry.

Depth & stereoTractable

Nearly free in a renderer, which is why depth-supervised models transfer well. Real depth sensors fail on shiny metal; simulate that failure too.

LiDARModerate

Ray casting with beam divergence, intensity return and dropout. Reflectivity of the actual material matters more than people expect.

ThermalHard

Needs a thermal solve, not a shader. Emissivity varies with oxide and roughness. Worth it in a steel plant, where thermal is often the only signal.

IMU & encodersEasy

Falls out of the physics step. Add realistic bias drift and quantisation or your state estimator will be optimistic.

Force–torqueHard

Contact-rich simulation is where sim-to-real most often breaks. Stiffness, friction and compliance need system identification against the real rig.

VibrationHard

Requires modal analysis rather than rigid-body physics. Usually cheaper to gather real data than to simulate it well.

Process signalsModerate

Motor current, pressure, flow. Best simulated from a plant model rather than a renderer, then fused with the visual stream.

The honest caveat

Synthetic data is not free accuracy. The sim-to-real gap is real, it is domain-specific, and a pipeline that never measures it is a graphics project wearing a lab coat. Every Mirror deliverable carries the same three numbers: performance on synthetic, performance on real, and the drop between them.

The second caveat is scope. F1 and F2 are achievable by one person in a quarter. F3 takes a real camera calibration and a real lighting survey. F4 needs hardware and a year. Anyone who tells you they built F4 in a hackathon built F1.

most of itself

Not "an AI company that runs itself" — that phrase hides a category error. Autonomy is not one dial; it is per-function, and a company is bottlenecked by its worst critical function, not its average one. A mill takes raw input continuously and rolls out product. That is the correct model: five engines that spawn, test and kill small products, with a human at the gate.

Human. Permanently.
Why it can't be fully autonomous Two walls

Error compounding. At 95% reliability per step, a twenty-step chain succeeds 36% of the time. At 99%, 82%. Autonomous operation over weeks means thousands of steps. No prompt fixes this — only verification gates that catch and retry, or short chains with checkpoints between them. Verification is the unlock, not model capability. That is why CP-03 sits underneath this project rather than beside it.

Legal personhood. A company needs a natural person who signs, is liable and passes KYC — a director, a bank account, filings someone is accountable for, a payment gateway that requires a responsible individual. The operational layer can approach full autonomy. The accountable layer cannot. Design for that instead of fighting it.

Ten visits a year is a hundred problem statements over a decade that exist nowhere else on earth. That is the moat, and it is the reason to run this in Jamshedpur rather than anywhere else.

09 Forge AI · ML · DL · CV

From scratch,
where it actually pays

"From scratch" means four different things with wildly different returns. Pedagogical — implement backprop once, then never again. Architectural — worth it only in constrained regimes. Infrastructural — your own harness, pipeline and eval stack, and the most underrated of the four. Foundational — pretraining a large model, which for a solo builder is arithmetic, not ambition. We do the third one, in one narrow domain, and use commodity models shamelessly everywhere else.

Stage 1

Harness

Weeks 1–8

Your own training loop, config system, deterministic seeding, checkpointing, and an eval harness reporting calibrated uncertainty rather than just accuracy. The thing you'll use for a decade. Don't skip it to reach models faster.

Stage 2

Data engine

Weeks 6–16

Ingestion from real industrial cameras. Physically plausible augmentation — lighting, scale, oxide, motion blur, modelled on the actual process. Active learning so an operator labels the right thirty images a week. The data engine is the moat, not the model.

Stage 3

Model line

Weeks 12–28

Anomaly detection with zero defect labels first. Then supervised detection where labels exist. Then sub-pixel measurement with an error bound. Written from scratch where the constraint demands it, borrowed where it doesn't.

Stage 4

Edge

Weeks 20–36

Quantisation, pruning, kernel fusion, target hardware profiling. Where CP-02 stops being theoretical. Ship to one real device on one real line, in the real ambient temperature.

Stage 5

Evidence

Ongoing

Every shipped model carries a failure analysis, a calibration curve, a drift monitor, and an honest statement of where it breaks. This is what separates an industrial system from a demo — and what a plant manager actually buys.

The niche: industrial vision in the no-data regime Where foundation models stay bad
Zero-label anomaly detection

The defect that matters happens twice a year. No examples exist to pretrain on, anywhere — so no amount of scale helps.

Metrology-grade vision

You need a measurement with an uncertainty bound, not a bounding box. Detection models have no calibrated error model at all.

Physics-informed vision

Constrained by what the metallurgy makes possible, not just by pixels. Requires domain physics inside the loss function.

Multimodal fusion

The camera is one of six sensors. Internet-scale pretraining data is overwhelmingly unimodal.

Hard-constrained edge

30 ms on a ₹15,000 box, at 55 °C, with vibration, and no cloud. Almost nobody optimises for this regime.

Never build yourself

Auth, payments, hosting, generic detection, OCR, embeddings, vector search. Every hour rebuilding a solved commodity is an hour not spent where nobody else can go.

15 Code How we behave

Ten rules,
enforced in the moment

Read aloud at every intake. The first four are adapted from Recurse Center's social rules, which remain the best-designed set in existence for a learning environment.

01
No feigned surprise

"You've never used a debugger?" Feigned surprise makes people hide what they don't know, which is the fastest way to kill learning.

02
No well-actually's

Corrections that add nothing but status. Correct things that matter, kindly.

03
No backseat driving

Don't lob advice across the room into a conversation you're not part of. Join properly or stay out.

04
No subtle -isms

Small demeaning remarks about background, college, employer, English, gender or caste. Called out lightly, apologised for lightly, moved on from lightly.

05
Show, don't announce

You may not present an idea. You present a thing that exists, however broken.

06
Publish or it didn't happen

Every session produces a public artifact. If there's no record, it didn't occur.

07
Contribution over attendance

Presence is not membership. Output is.

08
One layer down

When stuck, ask what the problem stands on, and work on that instead. On the stack diagram, that direction is down.

09
Credentials are invisible

Nobody's college, employer or title is mentioned in a technical discussion. What you built is the only relevant fact.

10
No spectators in the core

Ship every quarter or step back. Membership is regained the same way it was earned.

"Show me it running." "What does that stand on?" "How many layers do they own?"
